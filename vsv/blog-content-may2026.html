24hrs with a Local — keep all posts, optimise all, continue series

The 24hrs series is a strategic asset for internal and newsletter use — all posts stay. New posts publish 1–2 per month. The optimisation template below is applied to all existing posts by the Content Production Team to build E-E-A-T and organic reach without changing the editorial content.

Optimisation template — Content Production Team applies to all existing 24hrs posts

  • Rewrite title to lead with activity or place — not name + month. "A Day Fly Fishing Sun Valley's Big Wood River with Silver Creek Outfitters' Terry Ring" not "24hrs with Terry Ring, October"
  • Write a meta description for every post — 1 sentence: name + their angle + Sun Valley hook, 50–160 chars
  • Add named entity signals in body — specific trails, restaurants, and venues the local mentions by name (min 3 per post)
  • Add 2–3 FAQ questions at the bottom — "Where does [name] recommend eating in Ketchum?" — SGE snippet bait with 40–80 word answers
  • Add minimum 2 internal links per post — to relevant activity guides, dining guide, or seasonal pages
  • Add author bio or byline — E-E-A-T signal, even a one-line attribution works

SGE-RAISE™ framework — applied to all new posts and rewrites
R
Relevance Mapping

Map keyword to clear user intent: Exploratory, Informational, Transactional, or Navigational. Tone and depth follow intent type.

A
Answer Blocks

TL;DR or direct answer within first 100 words. H2/H3 phrased as questions. 40–80 word answer block per section. FAQ section at bottom of every post.

I
Entity Enrichment

Named places, trails, rivers, and venues throughout. Bald Mountain, Big Wood River, Argyros Center, Ketchum, Sawtooth — specific, never generic.

S
Structured Data

FAQ schema on all posts. HowTo schema on activity guides. Event schema where dates appear. Article schema baseline on all. Written in at creation for new posts.

E
E-E-A-T Signals

Author bio or byline visible. Date of last update shown. Named local knowledge. External references where relevant. First-person or experiential voice throughout.
