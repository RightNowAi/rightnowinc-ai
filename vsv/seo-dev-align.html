The core architecture question

Proposed two-layer content architecture

Layer 1 — Site pages (Dev owns)

  • /things-to-do/
  • /things-to-do/hiking/
  • /things-to-do/biking/
  • /things-to-do/fly-fishing/
  • /things-to-do/skiing-snowboarding/
  • /lodging/
  • /getting-here/

Layer 2 — Blog posts (Content owns)

  • Best things to do in SV
  • Mountain biking guide
  • Fly fishing guide
  • Skiing guide
  • Where to stay guide
  • How to get here

How they work together

  • Site pages = head term targets
  • Blog posts = long-tail support
  • Blog links up to site pages
  • Site pages link to relevant blog
  • No duplicate H1 / title tags
  • Schema stays on site pages

This architecture only works if we agree on it before both teams publish. If site pages and blog posts target the same primary keyword with similar content, Google will struggle to determine which to rank — and will likely rank neither well. The blog posts as currently written can be repositioned as supporting long-tail content with minor title/intent adjustments.

Questions for development team

3 decisions needed before either team proceeds on the overlap pages

  • 1
    Are the Priority 1 site pages being rebuilt with new content? Specifically /things-to-do/hiking/, /things-to-do/biking/, /things-to-do/fly-fishing/, /things-to-do/skiing-snowboarding/, /lodging/, and /getting-here/. If these pages are getting new editorial content targeting head-term keywords, the SEO team needs to know now — it changes the role and content focus of the corresponding blog posts already written.
  • 2
    Are the 9 conflict blog posts being used as link targets or content sources? If dev is building internal links or navigation that points to any of those 9 blog URLs, a 301 redirect from SEO will break those links. We need to know which URLs dev is actively referencing before we move them.
  • 3
    Can we agree on a two-layer architecture before publishing? Proposal: Dev site pages own the head terms (e.g. "mountain biking Sun Valley"). Blog posts own the long-tail and planning queries (e.g. "mountain biking trails Sun Valley Idaho beginner"). Blog links up to site pages. Site pages link to relevant blog posts. This prevents cannibalization and gives both layers a clear ranking role. SEO team can reposition the 10 new posts accordingly — most require only a title and first-paragraph adjustment.
