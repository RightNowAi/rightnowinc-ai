The one-minute version

Executive summary

  • More money, less budget. Paid + Grant search generated $73,140 in tracked donations — up 8.5% YoY — while real cash ad spend fell 15% to $18,656. Efficiency, not spend, drove the gain.
  • A net return, fees included. After ad spend and our $13,800 in annual fees, the paid program returned 2.25:1 — and roughly 3:1 once organic is counted. Most agencies never show this. We do.
  • One paid campaign is the engine. Plant-a-Tree Performance Max delivered $62,480 (85% of paid revenue) at a 4.57:1 return. December alone returned $28,678 — 39% of the year.
  • Organic is bigger than the dashboard admits. Analytics reported ~17,700 organic visits; Search Console (which can't be suppressed) shows ~52,300. We were seeing barely a third of our own organic — and it's growing.
  • The organic threat is AI Overviews, not rankings. We rank better and are seen 23% more, yet clicks fell 25% — because AI answers now resolve searches without a click. This is the story to get ahead of.
  • The Grant is reach, not revenue. $77,115 of free Google media drove reach and $7,010 in gifts — under-monetized, and the single biggest efficiency opportunity ahead.

New this year · Organic search

Organic: bigger than it looks, and shifting under our feet

Three independent sources tell one story — our analytics is undercounting organic, real organic is larger and rising, and the way people find us is changing fast.

What we saw vs. what actually happened

GA4 (consent-suppressed) vs. Search Console clicks (Google's own data — can't be suppressed)

Analytics captured just 34% of real organic — 66% under-reported, almost exactly matching our independent estimate. The hidden two-thirds is largely international reach (we're a global brand) — real authority, though not primarily U.S. donor traffic.

The AI Overview effect — the real organic story

Year-over-half change: we rank better and are seen more, but get clicked less

Impressions +23%, average position improved, yet clicks −25% and CTR halved. With 88% of our keywords now triggering AI Overviews, Google answers the query without a click. We're feeding the AI and getting fewer visits back.

The findings

What worked & where the upside sits

An honest read of the year — strengths to bank, gaps that point to opportunity. (The "how" is Phase 2.)

What worked

  • Efficiency over spend — more raised on 15% less budget; ROAS up 28%.
  • Net-positive after fees — 2.25:1 even with our costs counted.
  • Plant-a-Tree PMax — proven, scalable engine at 4.57:1.
  • Organic rankings improving — position up, non-branded discovery strong.
  • Higher-value donors — average gift up 12.5% to $69.79.

Opportunities identified

  • AI Overview click loss — ranking up, clicks down; the organic shift to protect against.
  • Grant monetization gap — $77k free media → $7k in gifts (0.09:1).
  • Organic measurement blind spot — 66% under-reported; no UTM path to credit it.
  • Revenue concentration — 85% of paid on one campaign; 54% of organic on one page.
  • Giving Search — 202 gifts but only $3.5k (0.80:1).

For the record

Methodology & data confidence

  • Paid/Grant dollars — high confidence. Donation revenue is Salesforce first-party data, matched by UTM. Consent-independent and reconciled to the ad platforms.
  • Attribution is last-touch UTM. Accurate for paid (ads always carry UTMs); structurally blind to organic (organic clicks carry no UTM), so organic revenue is understated and some is credited to "direct."
  • GA4 is consent-suppressed. A Consent Mode v2 setup is dropping non-consented sessions — GA under-reports organic traffic ~66% and total revenue ~50% vs. first-party sources. GA is used here for channel mix, not absolute dollars.
  • Organic traffic verified via Search Console (Google's own click data) and cross-checked against Semrush estimates. GSC is treated as truth.
  • Organic revenue is directional — a defensible floor ($23k–$27k), not an audited figure. Pinning it exactly requires the donor-platform total as an anchor.
  • Data hygiene note: a referral-spam/injection entry was found in the GA source list — recommend adding bot/referral filtering to the property.
