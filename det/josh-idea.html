Paid

The Ad Strategy

Organic first. Always. Ads are accelerant — not the engine. Only boost what's already working.

State Activation Ads

  • Runs 30 days before Josh arrives in each state
  • Geo-targeted: state-level, city + 50 mile radius
  • Creative: "Who should run with Josh in [State]?"
  • Audience: running interests + grief/loss interests + age 35-65
  • Goal: email capture via landing page

Content Amplification Ads

  • Triggered when an organic post gets traction
  • Boost the exact post that's working — never recreate it
  • Expands to lookalike audience built from email list
  • Goal: follower growth + email capture

Never boost: anything under 24 hours old, generic posts, or content with no organic engagement first.

Media

The PR Strategy

Local first. Always. National comes to you — you don't chase it.

Local TV stations are hungry, fast, and easy. "Local runner joining Josh Coleman on his 12,000 mile mission through [State]" is a segment they will run every time. Human interest + visual + feel-good = their content, not just Josh's.

50 states × local news = 50 credibility bricks. AP wire does the rest.

The only PR asset needed: One clean press release template written for a local editor with 4 minutes and a 3pm deadline. Not a PR document. A story they can steal with a local angle already written in.

Community

Engagement Strategy

This is a team sport. Josh quarterbacks. The team executes.

Josh Handles

  • All grief and Erin comments — no one else touches these, ever
  • Close friends and family — he knows who they are
  • Anything emotionally significant that requires his voice

The Team Handles

  • Mission content — miles, states, running, logistics
  • Nominated runners and local community
  • New followers finding the story for the first time
  • Inbound podcast and press inquiries

No generic thank yous. Ever. If you can't say something real, say nothing. Never respond as Josh unless you are Josh.

The Hater Strategy — Turn Haters Into Humor

Don't defend. Don't delete. Don't engage directly. Let the community vet it first. They will. The ultra running and grief communities have zero tolerance for people doing neither telling someone doing both that they're doing it wrong.

  • When the community delivers a perfect response — screenshot it
  • Post it
  • Josh adds one line

The hater becomes the content. The community becomes the hero. Josh stays above it. This is not a hater strategy. It's a community loyalty engine.

Crisis Response

This mission will attract people in genuine grief and crisis. That's not an edge case — it's inevitable.

Wendy and David are both certified Amen Clinics brain health coaches. The team can intervene with qualified, warm direction without creating liability.

  • Team monitors comments and DMs for genuine distress signals
  • Wendy or David respond directly — human, warm, qualified, not clinical
  • Josh gets involved only when his personal voice is the right tool

Still needed: A pinned response template that feels like the mission, not a disclaimer. A VA signal guide for what gets flagged immediately.

Revenue

How to Think About Sponsors

The mission is more valuable unsponsored than sponsored with the wrong partner.

Sponsorship is a slippery slope. Handle it wrong and the audience starts asking who's paying for Josh's grief. That's a question we never want them asking.

If a sponsor ever makes sense, they have to earn their place in the story. Not buy it. That means showing up in a state, supporting a nominated runner, or being something Josh already uses and would talk about anyway.

Could Fit — If Earned

  • Gear Josh already uses — shoes, apparel, nutrition, hydration
  • Mental health and wellness — therapy apps, grief support platforms
  • Van life and overlanding — he's living this for a year
  • Outdoor and adventure brands with authentic values

Never

  • Anything that trivializes grief or loss
  • Brands with no authentic connection to the story
  • Anyone who wants the logo more than the mission

The honest revenue sequence: Mission builds audience → audience buys books → books build platform → platform sells keynotes → keynotes fund the foundation. Sponsorship is not in that sequence right now.

Revisit in 2027 — after the mission completes and the documentary tells the full story.
